Meet our Guru

Shivakalamani. Ms. Sruthi Ganesapandian has been immersed in the sacred art of Bharatanatyam since the age of five. She learnt Bharatanatyam under the guidance of her esteemed Gurus, Smt. Abhirami, Shri S. Shivakumar, Smt. Sailaja Shivakumar, and Shri K. P. Karvarnan. Rooted in a strong traditional foundation, her journey in Bharatanatyam has been both an artistic pursuit and a path of deep spiritual growth. The title "Shivakalamani" was conferred upon Ms. Sruthi Ganesapandian in 2015 by her Guru, Shri S. Shivakumar, in recognition of her successful completion of a Diploma in Bharatanatyam.

Over the years, Sruthi has had the privilege of performing at prestigious festivals and revered temple stages across Tamil Nadu. Her notable performances include the Thillai Nataraja Temple in Chidambaram, Brihadeeswara Temple in Thanjavur, Arunachaleswarar Temple in Tiruvannamalai, and the Indian Dance Festival in Mamallapuram, along with many other significant cultural platforms. She has also performed at prominent temples such as Kapaleeswarar, Marundeeswarar, Ekambareswarar, and Nellaiyappar Temple in Tirunelveli, where her solo performance for the Thiruvempaavai Vizha earned her special recognition and numerous accolades. Additionally, she has showcased her artistry at venues including the ISKCON Temple and the Russian Cultural & Science Centre.

With time, her passion for Bharatanatyam evolved into a deep sense of responsibility to preserve and pass on this timeless art form. As a dedicated Guru, Shivakalamani Ms. Sruthi Ganesapandian has mentored and trained around 30 students, guiding them from foundational levels to advanced stages with a focus on both technical excellence and expressive depth.

In founding Natya Theerth Kalai Koodam in Halifax, she envisioned creating a nurturing space for Bharatanatyam in the Maritime provinces of Canada — a place where students of all ages and backgrounds can experience and embrace this rich cultural heritage. The name "Natya Theerth" (நாட்ய தீர்த்த) translates to "sacred waters of dance," symbolizing the purifying and transformative power of this classical art.

Her teaching philosophy is rooted in respect for tradition while honoring each student's unique journey. She believes that Bharatanatyam is not merely a dance form, but a discipline that fosters focus, creativity, cultural pride, and overall well-being. Her classes welcome students from age five through adulthood, from complete beginners to advanced performers preparing for their Arangetram.

For her, the most rewarding part of this journey is witnessing her students grow with confidence, discipline, and grace — carrying forward the legacy of Bharatanatyam with dedication and devotion.
